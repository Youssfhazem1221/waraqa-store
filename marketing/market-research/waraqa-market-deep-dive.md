## Glossary

- **GSM** — grams per square metre, paper weight. Higher = heavier, less likely to bleed with ink/marker/watercolor.
- **COGS** — cost of goods sold; what it actually costs to make one unit. Currently unknown for Waraqa (§11).
- **AOV** — average order value; the single biggest lever in §11's financial model.
- **CAC** — customer acquisition cost.
- **TAM / SAM** — total / serviceable addressable market (§1).
- **COD** — cash on delivery, Waraqa's only payment method today.

## 2. Growth & demand signals

- Egypt e-commerce GMV: **USD 9.91B (2025) → USD 11.49B (2026 projected)**, 10.0–11.89% CAGR through 2031.
- **Mobile commerce = 72.5–75% of transactions.** Waraqa's site should be judged primarily as a mobile experience.
- **Social commerce (direct IG/FB/TikTok transactions) ≈ 40% of online retail** — this is not a minor channel, it's close to half the market.
- Search term split (nationwide): **"دفتر رسم" 72%**, "sketchbook" 23%, "art journal" 5%. In affluent Cairo/Alexandria zones, "sketchbook" (English/transliterated) reaches near parity with the Arabic term — meaning Waraqa's urban target buyer searches in *both* languages, which raises the stakes on the bilingual-crawlability gap already flagged in the SEO audit (`marketing/seo/waraqa-seo-audit.md`).
- **Seasonality is bimodal and predictable:**
  - **Peak 1: September–October** — academic intake / back-to-school procurement.
  - **Peak 2: May–June** — graduation portfolio completion, summer creative projects.
  - Today is 2026-08-20 — **Peak 1 is 2–6 weeks away.** This is the single highest-leverage timing lever available for free (see Section 12).
- Physical retail is diverging: legacy wholesale (El Fagala) is squeezed by FX on imported paper margins, while premium chains like **Diwan Bookstore** are actively expanding stationery/journal shelf space into affluent suburbs (6th of October City) and resort towns (El Gouna) — a signal that premium-positioned paper goods have room to grow in Egypt's retail landscape, not just online.

## 4. Target customer & buying journey

- **Age 18–32**, concentrated in **Greater Cairo (48% of national e-commerce)** and **Alexandria (14%)**, with smaller pockets in Mansoura/Tanta.
- Segments: undergraduate creative students, practicing design professionals, self-taught hobbyists/bullet journalers.
- Income: **B/A/upper-middle bracket** — discretionary spend on non-commodity creative tools is real but not unlimited.
- **Discovery journey:** short-form video (Reels/TikTok) creates awareness → buyer evaluates on GSM, texture (hot-press vs. cold-press), and lay-flat binding → purchase via DM social commerce or a Shopify-style storefront, COD or mobile wallet.

```
01 Discovery         02 Evaluation        03 Purchase           04 Repeat
Short-form video  →  Spec-checking     →  DM or checkout     →  Word of mouth
Reels/TikTok          GSM, texture,        Social commerce DM,   Facebook groups,
stress-tests (§13)    lay-flat binding     or COD/wallet (§8)    campus crawls (§3,§12)
```

**Recurring pain points (verbatim themes from student forums/community threads):**

1. **Media bleeding & cockling** — sketchbooks marketed as "mixed media" locally often use sub-140gsm paper that bleeds with fineliner ink, alcohol markers, or light watercolor. *Waraqa's floor is 150gsm — already above this failure line on every SKU.*
2. **Fragile binding** — perfect-bound spines crack when opened flat; standard spiral notebooks deform in student bags. *Waraqa uses spiral/wire binding across the line — worth explicitly confirming Smyth-sewn or Wire-O construction where applicable, since the report flags this as a named quality marker buyers look for.*
3. **Import inflation** — EGP devaluation has pushed Canson/Fabriano/Moleskine to prices students can no longer justify.

**Willingness to pay by use case:**

| Use case | Price band |
|---|---|
| Daily rough sketch pads | 120–180 EGP |
| Finished mixed-media/illustration sketchbooks | 220–350 EGP |
| Specialized 300gsm watercolor journals | 400–650 EGP |

Waraqa's current 320gsm A5/A4 Mixed Media SKUs (220–250 EGP) sit in the *bottom* of the "finished mixed-media" band, and its heaviest paper (320gsm) doesn't yet reach the specialized-watercolor tier's 400–650 EGP band despite gsm that's in a comparable range to what commands that price elsewhere. That's real pricing headroom, not a typo (see Section 9).

## 7. Raw materials, sourcing & currency risk

- Production is a **hybrid model**: import fine-art paper stock, convert (print/die-cut/bind) domestically.
- Domestic mills (Rakta, Misr Edfu, Qena Paper Industry) produce newsprint/carton/low-gsm writing paper (60–80gsm) only — **fine-art paper (140–300gsm, acid-free, high-alpha-cellulose or cotton-blend) is imported** from Europe or East Asia.
- Converters (El Fagala, Gesr El Suez, Obour Industrial Zone) import parent reels/jumbo sheets and cut to size — **MOQ typically 500kg–1 metric ton per grade**, which is directly relevant to why small-batch production costs more per unit (see below).
- Real lay-flat performance requires **Smyth-sewn hardcover or Wire-O double-spiral binding** — standard perfect (glued) binding doesn't deliver it.
- **65–75% of a sketchbook's COGS is FX-exposed** (imported paper + foils). **A 10% EGP depreciation → ~6.5–7.5% COGS increase.**
- **Report's benchmark unit economics** (at-scale production, 280 EGP retail): COGS ≈ 90–110 EGP (35–40% of retail) → **60–65% gross margin** before fulfillment/CAC.

**This benchmark almost certainly does not apply directly to Waraqa's current batch.** The report's MOQ figures (500kg–1MT per paper grade) describe scaled conversion runs — a small handmade batch buys less paper, likely at a worse per-kg rate, and probably pays more per unit for die-cutting/binding setup that isn't amortized across thousands of units. Section 11 builds two scenarios (report-benchmark and small-batch-adjusted) rather than assuming the 35–40% figure applies as-is.

## 11. Financial strategy

**Headline finding: a single-item order acquired through paid advertising is likely unprofitable — bundle/higher-AOV orders are dramatically better.** This holds even under generous assumptions, and gets worse if the real courier cost exceeds the illustrative rate used below.

### Two COGS scenarios (since real cost data doesn't exist yet)

| Scenario | COGS as % of retail | Gross margin | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Report benchmark** | 35–40% | 60–65% | At-scale production, large paper MOQ (500kg–1MT/grade) |
| **Small-batch adjusted** | 50–55% *(illustrative)* | 45–50% | Assumes worse per-kg paper cost and unamortized setup cost at low volume — not measured, a placeholder to replace with real numbers |

Applied to current inventory (16,520 EGP retail value, 97 units):

| Scenario | Est. COGS | Est. gross profit |
|---|---|---|
| Report benchmark | ~5,780–6,610 EGP | ~9,910–10,740 EGP |
| Small-batch adjusted | ~8,260–9,090 EGP | ~7,430–8,260 EGP |

**This is before shipping subsidy and CAC — both matter enormously at these order sizes, as the next table shows.**

### Per-order economics — why AOV is the whole game

Assumptions: benchmark COGS midpoint (37.5% of item price), Cairo/Giza courier midpoint (95 EGP via Bosta), CAC midpoint (62.5 EGP), Waraqa's actual 50 EGP flat shipping charge. **All illustrative — replace with real COGS and real courier rate once known.**

| Order profile | Revenue | Est. COGS | Est. courier cost | Est. CAC | Net contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single Mini (120 EGP) | 170 (120 + 50 shipping) | 45 | 95 | 62.5 | **−32.5 EGP** |
| Single mid-tier item (200 EGP) | 250 | 75 | 95 | 62.5 | **+17.5 EGP** |
| 3-book starter bundle (~522 EGP, still under free-ship threshold) | 572 | 196 | 95 | 62.5 | **+218.5 EGP** |
| Free-shipping-qualifying order (~800 EGP) | 800 | 300 | 120 *(absorbed in full)* | 62.5 | **+317.5 EGP** |

**Read:** a paid-acquired single Mini order likely loses money once courier cost and CAC are counted. A 3-book bundle order is roughly 7x more profitable per order than a single cheap item — not because the bundle discount is generous, but because the fixed costs (courier, CAC) get spread across more revenue. This is the numeric backbone for the bundle recommendation already made in `marketing/pricing/waraqa-pricing-strategy.md` §3.

### Recommendations

1. **Get real COGS from your supplier before anything else in this section is trustworthy.** Everything above is a structured guess.
2. **Confirm the actual courier rate Waraqa pays** versus the 50 EGP charged to customers. If it's anywhere near the report's 85–150 EGP benchmark, single-item orders are being subsidized more than they can bear.
3. **Don't run paid Meta/TikTok ads against single-item cheap SKUs** (especially the Mini) until a bundle or AOV-lifting offer exists — per the table above, it's plausible those specific ads lose money on delivery, independent of ad quality.
4. **Hold off on marketplace listings** (Jumia/Noon/Amazon.eg, 15–22% referral fee) until real margin is confirmed — that fee comes straight out of a margin that isn't verified yet.
5. **Build a small FX buffer into the next production run's pricing** — 65–75% of COGS is import-exposed, and a 10% EGP move changes COGS by 6.5–7.5%. Consider one line of on-site copy that normalizes future price adjustments tied to import costs, consistent with the brand's existing "Fair Pricing" honesty positioning.

## 12. Sales strategy

### Sequencing, given a 97-unit bootstrap batch and a ~6-week runway to the Sept–Oct demand peak

**Zero-cost first (start immediately):**
- Reactivate the existing Instagram (3,000 dormant followers — a free, warm audience with zero CAC to re-engage). See Section 13 for the content plan.
- Launch TikTok. The report identifies Reels/TikTok as the primary discovery channel and best-ROAS format for this exact audience — Waraqa currently has no presence there at all.
- Join and genuinely participate in the two highest-fit Facebook groups: **Students of Fine & Applied Arts (~95k, highest purchase intent)** and **Cairo Sketchers/Urban Sketchers Cairo (~14k, ideal for portable formats)**. Don't build a new Waraqa Facebook Page from scratch — the report gives no evidence that format works here; the value is in the existing communities, not a new one.

**Low-cost, inventory-aware seeding (next 2–3 weeks):**
- The report recommends seeding ~50 free books to fine arts seniors/illustrators/TAs. **Scale this down to 6–10 units** — seeding 50 would be over half the entire current stock. Draw seeding stock from the **abundant SKUs (Mini, Kraft)**, not the scarce A5-150/A4-320.
- Prioritize creators/students already active in the Facebook groups above or matching the creator profiles in Section 3 (mixed-media, ink, or watercolor focus) — the report's content formats (stress-tests, flip-throughs) work best in their hands, not a generic paid ad.

**Hold until real numbers exist:**
- **Paid Meta/TikTok acquisition** — per Section 11, likely loss-making on single-item orders at current AOV. Revisit once a bundle exists and COGS is confirmed.
- **Marketplace listings** (Jumia/Noon/Amazon.eg) — referral fees eat into an unconfirmed margin.
- **University pop-ups/bazaars** — genuinely worth pursuing (Helwan, AUC, Cairo Design Week are all Cairo-based and feasible) but only if one falls within the current stock's runway; don't manufacture urgency to force-fit one in.

### Pricing sequencing

Don't reprice the current live batch — customers (including the 3k IG followers) may already know today's prices, and changing them now trades a small margin gain for a trust cost. Instead:

1. **Price the next production run inside the validated 240–340 EGP zone**, especially for 250–320gsm SKUs that are currently priced 60–130 EGP below comparable Bozoor/Alwan/Canson entries at similar specs (Section 6).
2. **Consider one new SKU at the specialized-watercolor tier (400–650 EGP, per Section 4's willingness-to-pay data)** to test genuine premium demand without disrupting the existing lineup — this is a test, not a lineup replacement.

### Profit-sharing framework

**Two separate things are being split, and conflating them is the most common way these arrangements go wrong:**

1. **Equity (ownership)** — permanent, doesn't change based on who's actively working this month. Reflects who founded the business and built its foundational assets.
2. **Active operator compensation** — variable, paid only to whoever is actively doing the work that period. This is what lets Abdelfattah's share stay fair *without* paying him the same as someone doing the daily work, and without cutting him out for a circumstance (conscription) that isn't a choice.

**Step 0 — before anything gets split:** hold back a reinvestment reserve (recommend **25–30% of net profit**, consistent with §11's recommendation to fund the next, better-priced production batch) and, if anyone personally fronted cash for the current 97-unit batch, return that capital to them first. **Open question I can't answer for you: who funded the current inventory?** If it was one person, they should get that specific amount back off the top before "profit" is calculated at all — otherwise you're distributing revenue that hasn't paid for itself yet.

**Recommended default equity split:**

| Person | Equity | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Abdelfattah | 35% | Co-founded the idea, created the brand identity, established manufacturer relationships — permanent, doesn't shrink because he's away |
| You | 45% | Co-founder; carries the largest ongoing operational and strategic load, including sole continuity during Abdelfattah's absence |
| Nermin | 20% | Not a co-founder from day one, but taking on a full, exclusive operating function (sales/marketing/digital) — a real stake, not just a salary, reflects that this is a family operation built on trust |

**Recommended active-operator pool split** (only among whoever is actively working that period):

| Person | Active pool share | While |
|---|---|---|
| Abdelfattah | 0% | In service — reassess and give him a share again once he resumes active work, even part-time |
| You | 60% | Broader scope: ops, tech, finance, fulfillment, strategy |
| Nermin | 40% | Sales, marketing, digital, customer-facing |

**How a profit cycle actually splits:** distributable profit (after Step 0) is divided into two pools — a **Founder Equity Dividend pool (40% of distributable profit)**, split by the equity table above regardless of who's actively working, and an **Active Operator pool (60% of distributable profit)**, split only among active workers by the table above. This is what lets Abdelfattah keep earning something real from equity while contributing zero hours, without diluting the two of you doing all the work.

**Worked example** — suppose a cycle nets **10,000 EGP** in distributable profit (after COGS, shipping, CAC, and the reinvestment reserve — see §11 for how to build toward a real number):

| Person | Equity dividend (40% pool) | Active pool (60% pool) | Total | % of profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abdelfattah | 1,400 | 0 | **1,400 EGP** | 14% |
| You | 1,800 | 3,600 | **5,400 EGP** | 54% |
| Nermin | 800 | 2,400 | **3,200 EGP** | 32% |

Abdelfattah earns a real, non-trivial share purely from what he already built, while the two of you doing 100% of the active work take home 86% of the profit between you — weighted toward you given the broader scope.

**Practical notes:**
- Revisit the active-pool split (not the equity split) whenever workload actually shifts — e.g., if Nermin ends up doing more than sales/marketing, or if Abdelfattah returns part-time during leave.
- Since Abdelfattah will be largely unreachable, put day-to-day and even most major decisions in your hands explicitly now, in writing, rather than needing his sign-off later when he can't easily give it.
- Even a one-page written agreement between the three of you (equity %, this pool structure, what happens if someone leaves) is worth doing before real money is flowing — it's far easier to agree on this now than to renegotiate later once profit exists to argue about.
