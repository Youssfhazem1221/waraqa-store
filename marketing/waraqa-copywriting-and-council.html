Copywriting Audit

Most of the existing copy is already good — this audit protects what works and flags four narrow, real issues.

Working well

Product descriptions name the exact media each paper handles

"perfect for watercolour, gouache, ink, and markers" (A5 Mixed Media) · "ideal for pencil, charcoal, and pastel work" (A5 Kraft). Benefit stated alongside the spec, not instead of it.

Working well

Checkout errors explain the fix, not just the failure

"Please enter a valid Egyptian mobile number (010, 011, 012, 015)" — exactly the standard to hold every other error message to.

Working well

Arabic copy is written, not translated

"خلّصت الكمية" and "هنكلمك عليه واتساب عشان نرتب ميعاد وصول المندوب" — real spoken Egyptian, not formal MSA. Rare for the category, and currently a wasted asset per the SEO audit's crawlability finding.

Fix

Hero CTA is vaguer than the featured-section CTA

hero.shopCta: 'Explore Sketchbooks' vs. featured.viewAll: 'See all 8 sketchbooks' — the second names a concrete number and is the stronger CTA by the skill's own formula.

Fix: change the hero CTA to "Shop All 8 Sketchbooks."

Fix

"Why We Started Waraqa" doesn't tell a story

The heading promises an origin narrative; the body underneath restates brand values already covered elsewhere on the page. No founder, no founding moment.

Fix: write the real story, or rename the section — and treat this as the seed for the content-strategy doc's "Behind the Paper" pillar.

Minor

Unused "Special Price" string signals an unshipped feature

common.sale: 'Special Price' exists in both languages but every SKU has compareAt: 0 — never rendered. Not user-facing, but worth flagging alongside the pricing doc's bundle recommendation.

Fix

The gsm explanation exists but isn't at the decision point

trust.ecoDesc already explains paper weight well — but only on the homepage trust row, not the shop grid or PDP where the buying decision actually happens. A placement gap, not a writing gap (cross-ref: pricing doc §1).

Positioning Council

Simulated council — each take is built from the advisor's published frameworks and positions, not their actual review of Waraqa.

The question before the council

Should Waraqa position narrowly around the "serious sketcher burned by cheap paper bleeding through" — the framing the comparison content already assumes — or pursue broader mass-availability marketing? How much should the handmade/price story carry the positioning versus reach and recall?

At stake: Waraqa is pre-launch — zero sales/traffic data exists. Whatever's chosen now shapes ad copy and content before it can be tested against real behavior.

Seated: April Dunford, Byron Sharp (dissenter), Eugene Schwartz, Rory Sutherland, Seth Godin

April Dunfordpositioning against real alternatives

The honest competitive alternative isn't another premium sketchbook brand — it's grabbing whatever's at the corner stationery counter, or adding a Jumia listing without checking gsm. That's indecision and inertia, which the comparison content already targets correctly rather than inventing a boutique rival.

The unique attribute (stated gsm + acid-free on every SKU) is concrete and provable against inconsistent marketplace listings; the value it enables is confidence, not homework. Reframe the market category from "sketchbooks" to "reliable heavyweight paper for wet media in Egypt" so the gsm guarantee becomes the obvious comparison point.

Bottom line: position narrow even if the audience reached ends up broader.

Byron Sharpdistinctiveness & penetration (dissenter)

Is "the serious ink/marker artist" a real, separable segment, or the same buyer as the casual one on a different day? The duplication-of-purchase law predicts heavy overlap — narrowing messaging risks excluding a much larger pool who'd still buy Waraqa if it simply came to mind at the right moment.

Also worth asking independent of the positioning debate: with 8 SKUs sharing a similar kraft-and-spiral look, would anyone recognize a Waraqa book with the logo removed? Distinctive assets may matter more long-term than any differentiation claim.

Bottom line: don't mistake a content angle for a market segment — build broad mental availability.

Eugene Schwartzawareness stage & mass desire

Most Egyptian sketchbook buyers are Problem-Aware at best — annoyed by a smear, not yet Product-Aware that Waraqa exists or that gsm is the variable at fault. The comparison page's spec-led TL;DR is right for a reader who's already arrived there — but anything reaching people earlier (ads, social) should open with the felt frustration, not the spec.

Bottom line: keep the comparison page spec-forward; write separate problem-first copy for top-of-funnel.

Rory Sutherlandprice & friction as signals

A price above the marketplace floor is a costly signal that makes the "handmade, inspect before you pay" story credible — matching Jumia's floor would undercut the story itself. The manual WhatsApp/COD confirmation step may look like inefficiency an optimizer would remove, but it's plausibly doing unmeasured work as proof of "checked by hand."

On the free-shipping threshold: try reframing what the fee signals (e.g. "Courier & Inspection Fee") alongside lowering the number, not instead of it.

Bottom line: the premium and the friction are working as signals — don't optimize either on efficiency logic alone.

Seth Godinsmallest viable audience

Is anything about Waraqa remarkable enough to be talked about without ad spend behind it? Probably yes — handmade-in-Cairo, inspect-before-you-pay is a genuinely different story than an anonymous marketplace SKU. Find the smallest group who's been burned by bleed-through, delight them completely, let them become the distribution mechanism before chasing reach.

Bottom line: earn a tribe's advocacy first; broad reach is the next phase, not this one.

Where the council disagrees

1. Sharp vs. Dunford/Godin — is the "ink/marker artist" a real segment or a content angle mistaken for one? Settled only by real purchase data: do self-identified serious buyers actually behave differently, or is it Sharp's predicted overlapping pool?
2. Schwartz vs. current draft copy — problem-first or spec-first? Settled by A/B testing a problem-led headline against the current spec-led opening on top-of-funnel channels.
3. Sutherland vs. the pricing doc — change the shipping fee's number, or its meaning? Settled by testing a reframed label against a lowered threshold, and against doing both.

Chair's synthesis

With zero real customer data yet, sequence rather than choose once: position narrow for now (Dunford, Godin) since that's the only version testable with content already built, while treating Sharp's reach argument as the next phase, not a rejection of this one.

  1. Ship the comparison pages as drafted — correctly positioned for a comparison-stage reader.
  2. Write a separate problem-first version for top-of-funnel/ad copy (Schwartz).
  3. Test Sutherland's fee-reframe alongside the pricing doc's threshold change, not instead of it.
  4. Hold off broadening messaging to "anyone who buys a notebook" until a tribe exists to broaden from.
  5. Tripwire: once order data exists, check it against Sharp's overlap prediction — if confirmed, retire the niche-only framing and shift toward broad mental availability.
